# Clause 10: Improvement

Clause 10 closes the loop. Problems found by audits, incidents, reviews or
complaints are fixed at the root, and the ISMS gets better over time. In the
2022 edition, continual improvement comes first (10.1) and nonconformity second
(10.2).

Part of [the audit-ready handbook](iso-01-certification.md). Previous:
[Clause 9](iso-07-clause-9.md). Next:
[Annex A.5: Organisational controls](iso-09-annex-a-5.md).

## 10.1 Continual improvement

**What it asks.** Keep improving how suitable, adequate and effective the ISMS
is.

**Documents.** None named. Evidence that improvement happens:

- findings closed, with the fixes verified,
- objectives met, and new ones set,
- measures trending in the right direction,
- management review decisions that led to changes,
- controls that have moved from partly to fully implemented.

**What the auditor looks for.** At a surveillance or recertification audit,
they compare this year with last year. An ISMS that looks exactly the same
three years running is not improving.

**In Assure.** Review **Get ready** each year. Raise improvements as **Tasks**.
The **Executive summary** report shows progress at a point in time; keep each
year's copy.

## 10.2 Nonconformity and corrective action

**What it asks.** When a nonconformity happens:

1. **React to it.** Take action to control and correct it, and deal with the
   consequences.
2. **Decide whether the cause needs removing**, so it does not happen again or
   somewhere else. To decide:
   - review the nonconformity,
   - find its cause,
   - check whether similar nonconformities exist, or could happen.
3. **Carry out the action** needed.
4. **Check whether the action worked.**
5. **Change the ISMS** if needed.

Corrective action should be in proportion to the effects of the nonconformity.

**Required records.**

- what the nonconformity was, and the actions taken,
- the results of the corrective action.

### Correction is not corrective action

| | Correction | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| What it fixes | This case | The cause, so it does not happen again |
| Example | Remove the access a leaver still had | Fix the leaver process so HR tells IT on the last day, every time |
| Enough on its own? | No | Yes, once it is shown to work |

Auditors mark findings as unresolved when only the correction was done.

### Finding the root cause

Ask "why?" until the answer is something you can change in the process, not a
person's name. "Alex forgot" is not a root cause. "Nothing tells anyone to do
it" is.

**What the auditor looks for.** Every finding from the last audit, internal or
external, with a root cause, an action, and evidence the action was checked and
worked. Findings from the previous certification audit are always followed up.

**In Assure.** **Findings**. For each one record:

- the type: observation, opportunity for improvement, minor nonconformity or
  major nonconformity,
- where it came from: internal audit, certification body, incident, customer,
- the root cause,
- the corrective action, with an owner and a date,
- how the fix was checked, and by whom.

A finding cannot be closed with nothing written down.

## Clause 10 checklist

- Every nonconformity recorded, with correction, root cause, corrective action
  and verification.
- No finding from the last certification audit left open without a plan.
- Evidence of improvement year on year.
