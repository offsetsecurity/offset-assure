# ISO 27001 certification playbook

ISO 27001 certifies that you run a working information security management
system, not that you bought security tools. Most organisations take four to
nine months. This is the path, and where each piece lives here.

Everything below is also in **Get ready**, which tracks how far you have got.
For the full detail on every clause, every Annex A control and every required
document, read the audit-ready handbook, starting with
[How certification works](iso-01-certification.md).

## Phase 0 — the management system paperwork (clauses 4 to 6)

| Deliverable | Clause | Where |
|---|---|---|
| What affects your security, and who your interested parties are | 4.1, 4.2 | ISMS → Context of the organisation, and Interested parties |
| ISMS scope | 4.3 | ISMS → ISMS scope |
| Information security policy | 5.2 | Policies (start from the template) |
| Roles and responsibilities | 5.3 | Controls → A.5.2, and your policy |
| Risk assessment method | 6.1.2 | ISMS → Risk assessment method |
| Security objectives | 6.2 | Objectives |

Auditors ask for these on day one, and a missing scope or method is the most
common Stage 1 finding.

## Phase 1 — what you protect, and what threatens it

**Assets** first: information, systems, devices, places, with owners. The
Sample library has over a hundred to start from.

**Risks** next. Score likelihood and impact, name an owner, choose a treatment,
and link each risk to the controls that reduce it. That link is what justifies
including a control later.

**Suppliers** count. Record what they hold and what assurance they gave, and
raise a risk where the answer is thin.

## Phase 2 — the Statement of Applicability (6.1.3)

Work through all 93 Annex A controls: does it apply, why, and how far along is
it. Write the reason for inclusions too, not only exclusions.

Watch the contradiction warning: a control you excluded that a risk points at
is the fastest way to lose an auditor's trust.

Export it from **Reports** and have it approved. It is the document they ask
for first.

## Phase 3 — do it, and prove it (clauses 7 and 8)

- **Policies** written, approved, versioned, with review dates.
- **Training** recorded: who, what, when (7.2, 7.3).
- **Evidence** attached to controls, dated, and refreshed when it ages.
- **Tasks** for every gap, with an owner and a date. That is your risk
  treatment plan being carried out.

## Phase 4 — operate it (what Stage 2 examines)

- **Incidents** logged and learned from (A.5.24 to A.5.27).
- **Findings** with root cause, action and verification (10.2).
- **Control testing** recorded, so controls can be shown to operate over time.
- **Calendar** checked weekly, so nothing quietly lapses.

## Phase 5 — audit yourself first (9.2, 9.3)

**Internal audit.** Plan it in Audits & reviews with a date; that entry is your
audit programme. Record what it found as findings.

**Management review.** Export the **Management review pack** for its inputs,
hold the meeting, and record the decisions.

Both are mandatory, and an auditor will ask for the records of at least one of
each.

## Phase 6 — the certification audit

| Stage | What happens | What you hand over |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Documents reviewed | Scope, policy, risk method, risk register, Statement of Applicability, internal audit, management review |
| Stage 2 | Evidence sampled, people interviewed | Evidence per control, test history, incidents, findings, training records |
| Surveillance | A lighter check each year | Fresh evidence, this year's audit and review |

Give the auditor an **Auditor** account in Users. They browse the evidence
themselves, and you skip days of document requests.
