# Using it day to day

Certification is not the hard part. Keeping the thing true between audits is.

## Once a week: the calendar

Open **Calendar**. Three groups: overdue, the next 30 days, after that.

Clear the overdue list, or change the dates and say why. A list of overdue items
nobody has looked at is the clearest sign to an auditor that the system is not
running.

## When something changes: the control

Changed a process, bought a tool, or fixed a gap? Open the control, update its
status and notes, and attach the new proof. Two minutes then, or an afternoon of
archaeology later.

## When you test something: record it

Open a control and use **Testing**: the date, the result, who did it, what you
found. A status is what somebody believes; a test is what happened on a day.
Auditors ask for exactly this when they want to see a control that operates.

## When something goes wrong: the incident

**Incidents.** What happened, how bad, where it stands. Afterwards, note what
you learned and what you changed. An empty incident log after a year usually
means nobody is reporting, not that nothing happened.

## When something is found: the finding

**Findings.** Record what was found, the root cause, what you changed, and how
you checked the change worked. That is clause 10.2 in four boxes, and it is
what an auditor follows from end to end.

## Every quarter: the evidence

Evidence older than 90 days is flagged. Re-collect it, or change what you are
claiming. Proof from two years ago proves what was true two years ago.

## Every quarter: risks and suppliers

Look at the top of the risk register and ask whether it still reflects reality.
Look at suppliers whose review date is close, and ask what they have shown you
lately.

## Twice a year: objectives

**Objectives.** Are you getting there? Record it. A measured objective that
moved is the best single slide in a management review.

## Once a year: the audit and the review

**Audits & reviews.** Plan the internal audit with a date, record what it found,
and raise findings. Hold the management review, export the **Management review
pack** for its inputs, and record the decisions.

Both are mandatory before certification, and both come back every year.
