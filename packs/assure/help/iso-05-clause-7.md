# Clause 7: Support

Clause 7 covers what the ISMS runs on: resources, skilled people, awareness,
communication, and control of documents and records. Most of the evidence an
auditor samples lives under this clause.

Part of [the audit-ready handbook](iso-01-certification.md). Previous:
[Clause 6](iso-04-clause-6.md). Next: [Clause 8](iso-06-clause-8.md).

## 7.1 Resources

**What it asks.** Decide what resources the ISMS needs, and provide them.
Resources means people, time, money, tools and outside help.

**Documents.** None named. Evidence usually comes from:

- management review decisions about resources,
- the security budget,
- contracts for outside help, such as an internal auditor or a managed
  security service.

**What the auditor looks for.** Whether the ISMS is plausibly resourced for its
scope. One part-time person running the ISMS for a large organisation raises
questions, and so does a long list of overdue tasks with no owner.

**In Assure.** Record resource decisions in the management review in
**Audits & reviews**.

## 7.2 Competence

**What it asks.**

- Decide what competence is needed by people whose work affects information
  security.
- Make sure those people are competent, through education, training or
  experience.
- Where they are not, act to fix it, such as training, mentoring or hiring,
  and check whether that worked.
- Keep documented evidence of competence.

**Required record.** Evidence of competence.

**What counts as evidence.**

- training records and certificates,
- CVs and qualifications on file,
- a competence matrix: each security role, the skills it needs, and who has
  them,
- records of on-the-job training or mentoring.

**What the auditor looks for.** They pick someone with a security role, such as
the person who administers the firewall, and ask for evidence that they are
competent for it.

**In Assure.** **Training** for courses and results. Attach CVs and
certificates as **Evidence** against Annex A controls 6.1 and 6.3.

## 7.3 Awareness

**What it asks.** Everyone working under your control must be aware of:

- the information security policy,
- how they contribute to the ISMS working, and the benefits of better security,
- what happens if they do not follow the ISMS.

**Documents.** None named, but awareness is tested by talking to people. The
auditor will stop someone in the corridor, or join a video call with them, and
ask: where is the security policy, how do you report an incident, what would
you do with a suspicious email? Records help you show that everyone was
reached.

**In Assure.** **Training** records who completed awareness training. On each
policy in **Policies**, **Who has acknowledged it** records who has read it.

## 7.4 Communication

**What it asks.** Decide what internal and external communication the ISMS
needs:

- what to communicate,
- when,
- to whom,
- how.

**Documents.** Not named as mandatory. A simple communication plan is the usual
answer.

**Examples.**

| What | To whom | When | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy changes | All staff | When issued | Email and intranet |
| Security incidents affecting customers | Customers | As required by contract or law | Named contact |
| Personal data breaches | The data protection regulator | Within the legal deadline | The regulator's process |
| ISMS performance | Top management | Quarterly | Report and meeting |
| Phishing warnings | All staff | As they happen | Email |

**In Assure.** **Communications**: what, to whom, when and how, with records of
what was sent.

## 7.5 Documented information

### 7.5.1 General

**What it asks.** The ISMS must include the documented information the
standard requires, plus whatever else you decide is needed for the ISMS to
work. How much depends on your size, how complex your work is, and how
competent your people are.

[Mandatory documents](iso-13-mandatory-documents.md) lists every item the
standard requires.

### 7.5.2 Creating and updating

**What it asks.** When you create or update documented information, make sure
it has:

- an identity: a title, a date, an author or owner, a reference number,
- a suitable format and medium,
- a review and an approval for suitability.

### 7.5.3 Control of documented information

**What it asks.** Documented information must be:

- available where and when it is needed,
- protected from disclosure, misuse and unauthorised change.

Where it applies, control:

- who receives it, who can access it, and how it is found and used,
- where it is stored and how it stays readable,
- changes, through version control,
- how long it is kept, and how it is disposed of.

Documents from outside the organisation that the ISMS needs, such as a
supplier's certificate or a regulator's guidance, must also be identified and
controlled.

**What the auditor looks for.** Documents with no version, no owner or no
approval. Review dates that have passed. Two copies of the same policy that
say different things. A master list of documents that does not match what is
actually in use.

**In Assure.**

- **Policies** holds every controlled document with version, owner, status,
  approver, approval date and next review. Changing the version keeps the old
  one in the version history.
- The **Document control list** report is your master list.
- The **ISMS master list of documents** template is a starting point if you
  prefer to keep the list in Word as well.
- **Evidence** holds records, each with the date it was collected.
- **Calendar** shows review dates coming up.

## Clause 7 checklist

- Resource decisions recorded.
- A list of security roles and the competence each needs.
- Evidence of competence for the people in those roles.
- Awareness training delivered and recorded, and policies acknowledged.
- A communication plan.
- Every controlled document identified, versioned, approved and in date.
- A master list of documents.
