# Clause 5: Leadership

Clause 5 is about the people at the top. The standard does not let an ISMS
belong to the security team alone: top management has to direct it, fund it,
and be able to talk about it. Auditors test this by interviewing them.

Part of [the audit-ready handbook](iso-01-certification.md). Previous:
[Clause 4](iso-02-clause-4.md). Next: [Clause 6](iso-04-clause-6.md).

## Who counts as top management

The person or group who directs and controls the organisation at the highest
level. For a certificate that covers part of an organisation, it is the people
who run that part. In a small company it is usually the managing director or
the founders. In a larger one it is the executive team or a named executive.

## 5.1 Leadership and commitment

**What it asks.** Top management must show leadership of the ISMS by:

- making sure the security policy and objectives exist and fit the
  organisation's strategy,
- building ISMS requirements into normal business processes, not running them
  as a side project,
- making sure the ISMS has the resources it needs,
- telling people why security management matters and why following the ISMS
  matters,
- making sure the ISMS achieves what it is meant to achieve,
- directing and supporting the people who make it work,
- promoting continual improvement,
- supporting other managers to lead security in their own areas.

**Documents.** None is named, but leadership leaves a trail:

- the signed or approved security policy,
- management review minutes with decisions in them,
- budget and headcount approvals for security,
- messages from leadership to staff about security,
- security on the agenda of board or executive meetings.

**What the auditor looks for.** Usually an interview with a member of top
management. Expect questions like: what is the ISMS for, what are our biggest
security risks, what did you decide at the last management review, and how do
you know the ISMS is working? A leader who cannot answer, or who says "ask the
IT manager", is a finding risk.

**In Assure.** Management reviews in **Audits & reviews**. Policy approval in
**Policies**. Leadership messages in **Communications**.

## 5.2 Policy

**What it asks.** Top management sets an information security policy that:

- suits the purpose of the organisation,
- includes security objectives, or a framework for setting them,
- commits to meeting the security requirements that apply to you,
- commits to continually improving the ISMS.

The policy must also be:

- available as documented information,
- communicated inside the organisation,
- available to interested parties where appropriate.

**Required document.** The information security policy.

**Keep it short.** This is the top-level policy: one to three pages, signed off
by top management. The detailed rules (passwords, access, backups) belong in
topic-specific policies, which come from Annex A control 5.1, not from this
clause.

**What the auditor looks for.** Approval by top management, a date, a review
date that has not passed, and evidence that staff received it. They may ask a
member of staff what the policy says, in their own words.

**In Assure.** Start from the **Master information security policy** template
in **Policies** → **Templates**. Record it in **Policies** with the approver and
approval date. Then use **Who has acknowledged it** on the policy to record
each person who has read it. That list is your proof that it was communicated.

## 5.3 Organisational roles, responsibilities and authorities

**What it asks.** Top management makes sure that the roles relevant to
information security have their responsibilities and authorities assigned and
communicated. Two assignments are required by name:

1. someone responsible for making sure the ISMS meets ISO/IEC 27001,
2. someone responsible for reporting ISMS performance to top management.

One person can hold both. Often this is the ISMS manager or CISO.

**Documents.** Not named as mandatory, but you need to show the assignments.
Usual evidence:

- a roles and responsibilities document, or a RACI table,
- job descriptions that mention security duties,
- an appointment letter or minute naming the ISMS manager.

**What the auditor looks for.** Named people, not job titles nobody holds. They
will check that the people named know they hold the role. Gaps and overlaps,
such as two people both believing they approve access, are findings.

**In Assure.** Put a named owner on every control, risk and policy. System roles
(administrator, contributor, auditor, read-only) are set in **Users**. Annex A
control 5.2 covers the wider security roles.

## Clause 5 checklist

- Top management identified, and able to talk about the ISMS.
- An information security policy, approved by top management, with a review
  date.
- Proof the policy reached staff: acknowledgements, training or a recorded
  briefing.
- A named person responsible for conformity with ISO/IEC 27001, and for
  reporting ISMS performance.
- Security responsibilities written into roles or job descriptions.
