# Clause 8: Operation

Clause 8 is where planning turns into doing. It is short, but Stage 2 of the
audit spends much of its time here, because it is where the auditor checks that
what you planned in clause 6 is actually happening.

Part of [the audit-ready handbook](iso-01-certification.md). Previous:
[Clause 7](iso-05-clause-7.md). Next: [Clause 9](iso-07-clause-9.md).

## 8.1 Operational planning and control

**What it asks.**

- Plan, carry out and control the processes needed to meet the ISMS
  requirements and to deliver the actions decided in clause 6.
- Set criteria for those processes, and control them against the criteria.
- Keep documented information to the extent needed to be confident the
  processes were carried out as planned.
- Control planned changes, and review unintended changes, acting to limit any
  harm they cause.
- Make sure that processes, products or services provided from outside the
  organisation, which are relevant to the ISMS, are controlled.

**Required record.** Documented information to the extent needed to show the
processes ran as planned. In practice: the records your controls produce.

**What this looks like.**

| Process | Its criteria | The record that proves it ran |
|---|---|---|
| Access reviews | Every system, every quarter | Signed-off review for each system |
| Patching | Critical fixes within 14 days | Patch reports with dates |
| Backups | Daily, restore tested quarterly | Backup logs and restore test results |
| Joiners and leavers | Access removed on the last day | Tickets showing access removed |
| Supplier reviews | Yearly for high-risk suppliers | Completed review forms |
| Change management | Every production change approved and tested | Change tickets |

**Outsourced processes.** If a supplier runs something for you, such as
hosting, payroll or IT support, you still own the control of it. Show the
contract terms, the assurance you received, and your review of it.

**What the auditor looks for.** Records, over time. One record from last week
proves the process ran once. A record every month for six months proves it
runs.

**In Assure.**

- **Controls**: each control with its status and owner.
- **Evidence**: records attached to the controls they prove, with the date
  collected. Assure warns when evidence is getting old.
- **Testing**: records of control tests, so you can show a control working over
  time.
- **Tasks** with kind **Change**: planned changes and their security impact.
- **Suppliers**: outsourced services, with the assurance each supplier gave.

## 8.2 Information security risk assessment

**What it asks.** Carry out risk assessments at planned intervals, and whenever
significant changes are proposed or happen, using the criteria set in 6.1.2.

**Required record.** The results of the risk assessments.

**What triggers an early reassessment.**

- a new system, service, site or supplier,
- a major change to an existing one,
- a serious incident,
- a new law, regulation or contract requirement,
- a change in the threats aimed at your sector.

**What the auditor looks for.** Evidence that the risk assessment is repeated,
not done once for certification and then frozen. Risk review dates that have
passed are a finding.

**In Assure.** **Risks**, each with a review date. Upcoming reviews appear in
**Calendar**. The **Risk register** report is the record of results.

## 8.3 Information security risk treatment

**What it asks.** Carry out the risk treatment plan.

**Required record.** The results of risk treatment.

**What the auditor looks for.** Treatment actions done, or in progress on a
reasonable timetable, and the residual risk reassessed once they are done. A
treatment plan that has not moved since last year is a finding.

**In Assure.** **Tasks** linked to risks and controls. When a task is done,
update the risk's residual score. The **Risk register** report and the
**Statement of Applicability** report together show the results.

## Clause 8 checklist

- Criteria set for each security process.
- Records showing each process ran, over months, not days.
- Outsourced processes identified and controlled.
- Risk assessments repeated on schedule and after significant changes.
- Risk treatment carried out, with residual risk updated.
